## Description

- A report has been published disclosing several denial-of-service and tracking vulnerabilities in the Bluetooth
specification. The report can be found here https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02905.
- The vulnerabilities and their impacts are summarized in the table below.

| Vulnerability # | Type of impact | BT122 | RS9116W | SiWx917 | CVSS Rating | CVSS Vector |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| B-E3 | passkey bypass | - | impacted | impacted | 7.6, High | CVSS:4.0/AV:A/AC:L/AT:P/PR:N/UI:N/VC:H/VI:H/VA:N/SC:N/SI:N/SA:N |

| B-E10 | denial of service | impacted | impacted | impacted | 7.1, High | CVSS:4.0/AV:A/AC:L/AT:N/PR:N/UI:N/VC:N/VI:N/VA:H/SC:N/SI:N/SA:N |

[1] See the researcher’s paper linked above for detailed descriptions of the vulnerabilities

### Fix/Workaround

- No fixes are available for the BT122.
- Fixes for the RS9116W are planned for later this year
- Fixes for the SiWx917 are planned for later this year
- Vulnerability B-E4 only applies to Legacy Pairing. As a best practice, use LE Secure Connections whenever possible since Legacy Pairing is already considered to be unsecure
