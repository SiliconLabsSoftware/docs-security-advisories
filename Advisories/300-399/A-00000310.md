# Silicon Labs Security Advisory A-00000310 : Debug port lock state is susceptible to fault injection attacks

Flags: MCU

CVSS Severity: Medium

CVSS Base Score: 6.8, Medium

CVSS Temporal Score: 6.6, Medium

CVSS String: [CVSS:3.1/AV:P/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:U/C:H/I:H/A:H/E:F/RL:U/RC:C](https://www.first.org/cvss/calculator/3.1#CVSS:3.1/AV:P/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:U/C:H/I:H/A:H/E:F/RL:U/RC:C)

## Product Impact

| Product | Impacted Version | Main SDK | Impacted Version | CVE |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SE Firmware | 1.2.6 and earlier | - | - | - |

### Source Product Impact Details

- EFR32xG22 or EFM32PG22 SoC’s and associated modules running VSE firmware 1.2.6 or earlier.

- All EFM32 and EFR32 Series 0 and Series 1 MCUs, SoC’s and associated modules.

- All EM35x SoCs and associated modules.

- RS9116, RS9113 and all other legacy Redpine Wi-Fi Connectivity ICs and modules.

- All EFM8 and C8051 MCUs and SoC’s.

- All other Silicon Labs products that do not specifically claim resistance to debug fault injection attacks.

- Note that EFR32xG21A/B (all SE versions), EFR32xG22 and EFM32PG22 (with VSE versions 1.2.7 and above), and all other Series 2 EFR32 and Series 2 EFM32 products have been hardened against debug fault injection attacks and are robust against them.

## Description

- A precisely timed fault can be injected during the device boot process that allows debug access to read and write device memories and peripherals even though the debug port was correctly configured to its “locked” state. This attack bypasses the standard debug unlock side-effect that erases the device flash and RAM memories.

- These types of attacks require physical access to the device and can often be accomplished with inexpensive tools and a minimal time investment.

### Fix/Workaround

- EFR32xG22 or EFM32PG22 SoC’s and associated modules can upgrade to VSE firmware 1.2.7 or later. VSE upgrades can be performed via the debug port or via over the air. VSE upgrade instructions can be found in Chapter 5 of [UG266: Silicon Labs Gecko Bootloader User’s Guide](https://www.silabs.com/documents/public/user-guides/ug266-gecko-bootloader-user-guide.pdf).

- For all other impacted products, preventing this attack requires either isolating the device from physical access or redesigning the product to use a non-susceptible component.

## Background

- Fault injection attacks against the debug interfaces on programmable MCUs have become more prevalent in recent years due to the evolving product security landscape. Knowledge of these types of attacks and professional tools and techniques to accomplish them have become more pervasive and less expensive over time [1]. Silicon Labs began a lengthy investigation of the susceptibility of our Series 1 EFR32 and EFM32 products to debug fault injection attacks in 2020 which resulted in this security advisory disclosure.

- Increasing the robustness of a device to fault injection attacks in hardware is possible if the requirements to do so are known during the design phase of the device. Unfortunately, devices and products that were designed before such attacks were commonplace, which includes many Silicon Labs product families listed in this advisory, are often susceptible to them. We recommend that customers assume that any devices that do not specifically claim fault injection resistance will be susceptible to these types of attacks, regardless of vendor.

- Series 2 Secure Vault Mid and Secure Vault High products, including the EFR32xG21A and B and EFR32xG22 (running VSE firmware 1.2.7) have been evaluated by two independent security labs. These labs have assessed the devices and determined that the debug interface implementations are robust against fault injection attacks. Silicon Labs can supply these reports for review under NDA.
